Busy Bees Test Site Walkthrough — Step-by-Step Guide

What this is: a checklist for trying out the new Busy Bees board before it goes live. Go in order, top to bottom. Tick off each part as you finish it. When every part is ticked, this walkthrough is done — Bobby takes your notes from there and decides when the new board goes live.

Before you start — read this first

  • The test site is a practice copy, not the real site. Nothing in it is real: no real customers, no real contractors, no real texts, no real payments. The names, jobs, and numbers in it are made-up examples.
  • Open the test site from the review page — button 2, "Open the test site." It opens in the phone layout, which is how most people will see it.
  • You can't change the test site yourself — and you don't need to. When you spot something that should change, write it in your notes in plain words ("on the cart screen, the button is hard to tap") and send the notes to Bobby.
  • Jot problems down as you find them, or save up a list. Either way works. If something looks broken, note what you tapped right before it broke.
  • Don't worry about these — they don't go live: the Dispatcher view link on the home page (it's a test link, marked as one), the made-up example data, and the map box picture (see Part 3). Judge the flow and the wording, not those.

Plain words used in this guide:

  • Cart = the list of jobs you're asking about, before you send anything.
  • Lead fee = what a contractor pays Busy Bees when they accept a job. Customers never see fees and never pay them.
  • Roster = the approved list of contractors on the board.
  • Dispatcher view = the owner-only screen — Bobby's screen. You get to look at it in this walkthrough. Real customers and contractors never see it.

Part 1 — Customer flow, as a guest (no sign-in)

This is the most important part. A guest is someone who just opens the site and asks for work, without an account.

  1. Open the test site. You land on the preview home page: a "Give us a buzz" welcome, two big buttons (Request a local pro, and I'm a contractor), a short "How it works" card, and below that a menu in three groups — Customers, For contractors, and Behind the scenes. The areas live under "Areas we serve" in the Customers group.
  2. Tap Request a local pro.
  3. Pick a town from the town grid.
  4. Pick a category (there are 7: Tree Work, Lawn Care, Landscaping, Storm Cleanup, Snow Removal, Heavy Hauling, Pressure Washing).
  5. Pick a job inside that category.
  6. If it asks for a size, pick one.
  7. Fill in the details screen (name, phone, notes).
  8. Look at the confirmation screen. It should show a big text button and make it obvious this is a request, not an instant booking — the pro confirms first. (In the test site the text is only pretend. Nothing is actually sent.)

Check as you go:

  • Every screen has a Back button and a Home link in the header.
  • You can tell where you are on every screen (the path cue).
  • No screen makes you scroll forever to find the button.
  • No screen has more than 8 choices on it.
  • The words are plain and the buttons are big enough to tap easily. Think: could an older person do this without help?
  • The confirmation screen has a Back to home link.

Do this whole guest flow at least once. Then repeat steps 2–8 for one job from each of the other 6 categories — you don't need every single service, just one per category, so all 7 get covered.

Part 2 — Customer flow, signed in (contractor choice)

Sign in as the pretend signed-in customer (the test site gives you a pretend signed-in state — no real account needed).

  1. Go through town, category, job, and size again.
  2. At the end of picking the service, you should get asked which contractor you want. Try each of the three choices:
    • A previous contractor (someone you've used before).
    • A site-recommended one (picked by availability, capacity, rating, and verified first-look).
    • Discover one — this shows a top 5 list, and also offers a random matching contractor. Random only ever means a contractor who matches the service, the town, and has room — never the wrong trade.
  3. Finish the request and check the confirmation screen again.

Check as you go:

  • The three choices are clear and easy to tell apart.
  • Guests never see this question (they go straight to recommended routing).
  • Nothing here promises a contractor is available or how fast they'll come.

Part 3 — Size screens: calculator, square feet, and the map box

Size is where jobs get their price tier, so try all three ways of giving a size.

  1. Pick a size from the list. Try a job with size bands (for example mowing or mulch) and check the band words make sense.
  2. Type a square footage. Look for "Know your square footage? Enter it" on a size screen, type a number, and check the board puts it in the right tier. Boundary rule to know: an exact boundary number rounds DOWN to the lower tier.
  3. Open the map box. On a size screen where it appears, tap to open it, trace an area, and check the square-foot number fills into the field. Important: the map picture in the test site is a schematic lot grid, not real satellite photos, because the test site has no live map connection. The real site gets real satellite imagery to trace. Judge whether the box opens, traces, fills the number, and closes without breaking the flow — not the picture itself.
  4. Check the tips and reference sizes on the size screen (for example, a parking space is about 160 sq ft, and 1 yard of mulch covers about 100 sq ft).

Check as you go:

  • Typing a number always works, even if the map box fails (manual fallback).
  • The size words say "area to work, not lot size" where it matters.
  • The map box never slows down or blocks the main flow.

Part 4 — Cart (more than one job at once)

  1. Add a job to the cart, then go back and add a second, different job.
  2. Open the cart (top-right corner of the screen).
  3. Check every item shows which service, which variant, and which size — not just the group name. For example, if you picked a grouped button like "Driveways, patios & decks," the cart must say which one you actually picked.
  4. Check the cart total adds the jobs up.
  5. Check the confirmation tells you, in plain words, that each job goes to recommended contractors based on availability, job size, site rating, town and service match, verified first-look, and continuity.

Check as you go:

  • Cart button is in the top-right corner, on phone and on every screen.
  • Each cart item shows its exact variant and size.
  • The cart never shows a fee to the customer.

Part 5 — "Something else? Tell us"

  1. Find the Something else? Tell us link under the category grid.
  2. Describe a job in your own words, add name and phone, and send it.
  3. Check that no price tier is shown for it — Bobby assigns the tier later, and the contractor confirms it before anything is paid.

Check as you go:

  • The link is easy to find, both under the categories and at the bottom of service screens.
  • Nothing here invents a service or a price for a job you described.

Part 6 — Wording pass (whole site)

Go back over the screens you've already tried, this time reading only the words.

Check as you go:

  • The main buttons say Request a local pro, not vague words like "Get service" or "Book a service."
  • A contractor's own page says Request this contractor plus the service.
  • Under the main button, a helper line says it's free to send, the pro confirms first, and it's a request, not an instant booking.
  • Nothing says "emergency," nothing promises a fast response, and nothing claims anyone is insured.
  • No customer screen shows a fee, a dollar amount, or the word "subscription."
  • Bobby's separate contracting business name (Bob of All Trades) and Bobby's personal name appear nowhere customer-facing.

Part 7 — Contractor side

The test site has 3 pretend contractors. Try all three, because they show different states:

  • Contractor A: verified with both proofs (licence + insurance), higher level.
  • Contractor B: insurance only, mid level.
  • Contractor C: unverified ("not checked"), new, low level.

For each one:

  1. Open the contractor view and look at their profile.
  2. Open their lead inbox. Accept one lead and pay it in test mode (pretend money only — nothing is charged).
  3. Decline or pass on another lead. Check that passing costs nothing and the job moves to the next contractor.
  4. Open the contractor job board. It must show service details only: service, town, size/tier, timing, job description, lead fee. It must NEVER show a customer name, phone, email, address, or map pin.
  5. Try the I'm interested button on a board listing. Check it feeds into routing — it does not let anyone jump the queue or claim the job directly.
  6. Check the job statuses read open / held / filled, and that a held job shows a countdown.
  7. After an accept + pay, check the masked relay: the contractor gets the address and job notes, but NOT the customer's phone number.
  8. Look at the ratings pages: the public side shows stars and approved comments with business name, service, town, and customer first name + town only. Google reviews, if any, are a link out — never part of the ranking.

Check as you go:

  • Verified contractors get first look with a countdown; if they don't answer in time, the job passes on with no penalty.
  • Standing-accept (a job held for a contractor to approve) only exists for verified contractors who opted in, and only on small jobs.
  • Unverified contractors are labelled "not checked" — never hidden, never described as bad.
  • New contractors start neutral ("New to Busy Bees"), never penalised for having no history.
  • No contractor screen shows the dispatcher/owner view, fees for other contractors, or any customer list.

Part 8 — Contractor Pages and membership (new)

This is the newest part: contractors get their own page for their own customers.

  1. Open the Contractor Pages screen.
  2. Look at a member's page: a shareable link (busybeesdispatch.com/plus their name) and a QR placeholder to share.
  3. Open a member's page as if you were their customer. Pick a service. Check the request flow shows a Your contractor banner with that contractor locked in, and skips the contractor-choice question.
  4. Check the contractor still gets notified, and it's still a request with confirm-before-pay and masked contact — not an instant booking.
  5. Look at the membership + 10% ledger on a pretend contractor: members pay a membership PLUS 10% of the normal tier fee on each accepted own-page booking only (10% of $5 is $0.50, of $25 is $2.50, of $50 is $5, of $100 is $10). The 10% amounts add up and are billed once with the membership, not charged one by one.
  6. Try the breakeven calculator. Type in a membership price, an average fee, and a number of jobs, and check the maths makes sense — including the quiet month with 0 or 1 jobs, where membership costs more than pay-per-job.
  7. Check board-matched jobs (jobs the board routes, not from a contractor's own page) still cost the FULL tier fee for everyone, member or not.
  8. Check membership never buys ranking, rating, level, or verified status.

Check as you go:

  • The member page flow is as easy as the normal customer flow.
  • The plan-switching controls on pretend contractors are clearly just pretend controls — they don't go live.
  • The prices on the page (Weekly $19 / Monthly $49 / Yearly $490) are the real, decided prices — Bobby set them on October 27. Membership itself is not for sale yet (a legal review comes first), so your job is just to check the maths and the wording.

Part 9 — Dispatcher view (owner only — Bobby's screen)

This is Bobby's screen — you're getting a look behind the counter. Contractors and customers never see it.

  1. Open the dispatcher view and look at the routing queue.
  2. Look at the suggestion cards. These are design-only ideas — nothing here applies itself. Bobby decides everything.
  3. Look at the referral and discount tracking, and the decision log (a record of past calls, so future suggestions can learn from them).
  4. Check the queue shows whether each customer is registered (signed in) or a guest.

Check as you go:

  • The queue is readable at a glance: what the job is, where, what tier, who it's with, and what state it's in.
  • Nothing on this screen is reachable from a customer or contractor screen.
  • The page is clearly marked as owner-only.

Part 10 — Areas and pre-launch pages

  1. Open Areas we serve from the home page's Customers group and check it works as an area directory.
  2. On the Cape Cod (live) area, check the 8 towns are right: Yarmouth, Hyannis/Barnstable, Dennis, Sandwich, Chatham, Harwich, Mashpee, Brewster.
  3. Check the three pre-launch areas on the same page:
    • South Shore: should show 2 of 3 contractors on the roster (a pretend count), NOT live yet.
    • Western Mass: should show 1 of 3 (a pretend count), NOT live yet, and no going-live date — never a made-up date.
    • Rhode Island: should show 2 of 3 (a pretend count), NOT live yet.
  4. Check the Yarmouth + surrounding counter is hidden while testing.
  5. Check every pre-launch page says plainly: sign-up is open, the area goes live only when 3 approved contractors are on the roster, and the date moves if 3 aren't reached (no empty boards, ever).
  6. Check each area has its own look (its own skin) but the same deal, flow, tiers, and fees underneath.

Check as you go:

  • Every counter shows real approved-roster numbers only — never fake numbers.
  • Town lists on pre-launch pages are labelled "Proposed towns — to be confirmed" where they aren't final.
  • The Home link works from every area page.

Part 11 — Suggestions page and Home link sweep

  1. Open the Suggestions/Contact page. Send a test suggestion. Check the form is simple (what it's about + a message + optional name) and that suggestions are private to Bobby by default.
  2. Go through every screen you can find and check the Home link is in the header on every single one, and the confirmation screen has Back to home.

Part 12 — Desktop pass (Bobby's laptop check — you can skip this part)

This part is Bobby's own check, done later on his laptop after the phone pass — you can skip it. For the record: on a wide screen, only the arrangement gets checked (columns, spacing, layout). Desktop-only arrangement tweaks must NOT change the phone view. The words, order, and services are shared across both, so a wording change on one would change the other too.

Part 13 — Bobby's decisions at the end

These are open items for Bobby to decide — not for you. They're listed so you know what's still unsettled, like the placeholder prices in Part 8. You don't need to pick anything. If you have an opinion on any of them, put it in your notes.

  1. Membership prices — decided. Bobby set them on October 27: $19/week, $49/month, $490/year. Membership is not for sale until the legal review clears. (Also settled: the 10% applies to own-page bookings only; board-matched jobs cost the full flat fee.)
  2. Range fees. Some fees are ranges (for example tree trimming $15–$30). Every range endpoint needs a value from the 13 Stripe amounts ($5–$100), unless Bobby approves the rule that the dispatcher may use any of the 13 amounts.
  3. Snow flow fix + plow split. Bobby to approve: putting options before size in the snow flow, and splitting the plow options so no screen has more than 8.
  4. Bush seasonal routing + roof-wash fee. Bobby to approve: routing bush jobs through the frequency screen, and setting the roof-wash fee.
  5. Spec values (proposed, not final). Level bands (New 0–4 / Growing 5–19 / Established 20–49 / Trusted 50+), approve-window minutes (15 / 30 / 45 / 60, 1 hour max), cart level discount (Trusted 10% / Established 5% / else 0%), and peak promo windows. Bobby says which he keeps and which he changes.
  6. Whole-property vs cart-sum rule. A whole-property cleanout is one fee, but adding house + driveway + deck separately in the cart sums higher. Bobby rules which one wins when they overlap.
  7. Attorney review. Bobby approves the Terms Rev 3 direction, and a Massachusetts attorney reviews them before anything launches.

When you're done

You are done when:

  • Parts 1–11 are all ticked.
  • Every problem or idea you found is written in your notes, and the notes are sent to Bobby.
  • Any opinions you have — including on the open items in Part 13 — are in your notes.
  • Part 12 is skipped — that's Bobby's own laptop check.

Then tell Bobby you're done and send him your notes. What happens next is Bobby's side: when he's happy with everything, the new board goes live behind a maintenance page — maintenance page up, new board swapped in and checked, maintenance page down, old site ready to put back if anything looks wrong.

Part 14 — Almanac + sponsor ads (added Oct 30, 2026)

The free gardening section and the sponsor cards went into the test site on Oct 30. Check them like this:

  1. Open The Cape Cod Yard Almanac from the home page's Customers group. It must open with NO sign-in wall — anybody can read it.
  2. On the Almanac page, count the sponsor cards: 2. Cards must say "Sponsored — Local Partner" and show garden-type stores only (nurseries, garden centers, hardware) — never a contractor.
  3. Open the one finished article, "When to do your fall cleanup." It has 1 sponsor card, and it ends with ONE calm button pointing to "Request a local pro" — nothing pushy, no pop-ups, nothing moving. The other topics on the Almanac page are marked "Coming soon" — they should NOT open the fall-cleanup article.
  4. Run one full request (guest) start to finish and confirm: zero ads anywhere in the request flow.
  5. Anything off — wrong count, a card in the wrong place, wording that feels pushy — note the screen name and put it in your notes for Bobby, like the other parts.

Done when: counts check out on the Almanac page and the one article, and the request flow is clean.
