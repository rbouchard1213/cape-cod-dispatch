A walkthrough + checklist for Busy Bees reviewers — in plain words

What you're looking at

Busy Bees is a local service dispatch board for Cape Cod. Homeowners request yard and home services for free. Contractors get a text about the job and only pay a small flat fee if they say yes to it.

The test site — button 2 on the review page — is the test version of the site. Everything in it is pretend — fake customers, fake contractors, pretend texts, pretend payments. Nothing you tap is real, and you can't break anything. Poke everything.

Two things that look like mistakes but aren't:
  • Different fees. In the test site, contractor fees run $5 to $100, set by the size of the job — that's the new pricing. The public site (busybeesdispatch.com) still shows the old $25–$35 fees until the new site takes over on November 13. Both are right for now — please don't flag the difference.
  • Membership and Premium are pretend. The test site shows contractor membership and a customer "Premium" plan ($20/month after a 30-day free trial). Neither one is for sale yet — both are waiting on a legal review. Judge the wording, not the buying.
What Busy Bees needs from you: fresh eyes. You're not being tested — the site is. If something confuses you, makes you stop and think, or looks wrong, that's exactly what Busy Bees needs to hear. "I got lost here" is one of the most useful things you can say.
The deal: Busy Bees pays $30 when you've gone through the whole guide and checklist and sent your notes back — your choice: $30 cash, or a $35 gift card. Pay comes in full, all at once when your finished notes are checked in. Finish by Friday, November 13, 2026. Finish by Friday, November 6, 2026, and that's an extra $10. Find something real that is not on the checklist: $5 for each one (extras top out at $25 per person). One truly exceptional catch earns a $20 bonus on top. (Busy Bees decides what counts as a real find — the same thing found twice counts once.) Write your review code (from the message Busy Bees sent you — like BEE-01) at the top of your notes, so your review gets matched to you.
Reviewing as a contractor? Your pay is different. Regular reviewers get the $30 cash or $35 gift card above. If you're a contractor, you don't get the cash or the gift card — your review pay is one free month of Busy Bees membership (a $49 value) instead. It starts on its own, and if you're already in free membership time, the month banks and waits for you. Everything else works the same for you: the extra $10 for finishing by Friday, November 6, 2026, and the find bonuses.
Two codes, two different jobs. Busy Bees uses two code systems that look alike but are not the same thing. BEE codes (like BEE-01) are review codes — one per reviewer. Your BEE code opens the review page, goes at the top of your notes, and ties your review to your review pay. BUZZ codes (like BUZZ-01) are founding codes — one per founding contractor. They go on that contractor's flyers, and the free membership days for an approved recruit credit to that code. A BEE code never earns founding credit, and a BUZZ code never counts as a review. One person can hold one of each — they're kept on two separate lists.

How to start

  • The first time you open the review page, a reviewer agreement pops up. Type your name and your review code (from the message Busy Bees sent you — like BEE-01) and tap "I agree". That lets you in, and your device remembers it, so you only do it once.
  • Use the same device and the same browser for the whole review. Your agreement is remembered on the device where you tapped "I agree" — switch phones or browsers and the site will ask you to agree again, and you'll be checked in a second time. Start on your phone? Finish on that phone.
  • Go back to the review page and tap 2 — Open the test site. It opens right here in your browser, on a phone or a computer — Chrome works best (Edge, Firefox, and Safari are fine too).
  • If you can, do it on your phone — that's how most people will use the real site.
  • The test site's home page is a menu in two groups. Customers — the request flow, your account, a member's own page, the areas page, and the Yard Almanac. For contractors — how job offers work, a pretend job offer, membership, levels & verified, founding codes, and a "Don't see your trade?" page for trades Busy Bees doesn't carry yet. The two passes below walk you through one group at a time.
  • Jot down the time you start. The notes sheet asks for your start and finish times when you're done — any long breaks, just note them; they don't count against you.

The walkthrough — two passes

Pass 1 — Pretend you're a homeowner

  • Start a service request. Pick a town, pick a service, answer the questions one screen at a time.
  • When it asks about size, try the map/box tool and the square-foot entry.
  • Go all the way to the final confirmation screen. Read it like a stranger would.
  • Open Your account — free & premium from the home page. That's the signed-in customer's home: saved details, past requests, one-tap re-requests. The Premium extras are pretend — Premium isn't for sale yet.
  • Then look at the Yard Almanac section (yard tips and seasonal guides). It should open right up — no sign-in.

Pass 2 — Pretend you're a contractor

  • From the home page, use the For contractors group. Start with Join & how job offers work — it explains the offer text a contractor gets, the flat fee, and what happens if a job falls through: the fee can come back as account credit (credit sits on the account, never expires, and is never paid out as cash).
  • Open See a pretend job offer. There are two sample offers: a one-time job and a weekly repeat job. On a repeat job, the fee is paid once per arrangement, never per visit (one arrangement = one customer, one address, one kind of work).
  • Tap Accept on one sample and read what happens next. Then go back and tap Decline on the other. Declining is always free and never counts against the contractor.
  • Read Membership — your own page and Levels & verified carefully — the checklist below asks about them. "Verified" means a contractor showed Busy Bees some business paperwork — a license, a town business certificate, or insurance — and Busy Bees keeps a copy on file. Customers see that badge as "Paperwork shown." Busy Bees does not run background checks — flag anything that sounds like it does.
  • Open Founding codes. It shows an example founding code (BUZZ-01) and explains the founding offer. There is nothing to type in — the test site only explains the codes; it doesn't take one anywhere.
  • Open Don't see your trade? — the application process for trades Busy Bees doesn't carry yet (the examples are auto repair and HVAC). A contractor can apply to a waitlist, but no new trade is live, applying is free and promises no jobs, and nothing opens on its own: Busy Bees reviews every application personally, and a trade is normally only designed once three approved contractors offer it in the same area. The screens also explain the back-office AI — it only sorts and counts applications; it never approves anyone, contacts anyone, or adds a trade.

The checklist — things that must be true

Tick each one off as you confirm it. If anything below is not true anywhere you find it, that's a flag — note it and tell Busy Bees.

Words and numbers

  • The name is always Busy Bees — both B's capital, no apostrophe. Flag "Busy Bee's" or lowercase versions.
  • The only phone number anywhere is (508) 258-9899. Flag any other number, anywhere.
  • There is no "emergency" service and no emergency wording anywhere.
  • No home address or street address for the owner or the business appears anywhere.

Money rules

  • Homeowners are never shown a fee and never asked to pay anything.
  • No screen asks a homeowner for a card or payment details.
  • Contractors pay only when they accept a job. Declining or ignoring a job is always free.
  • Fees are flat amounts between $5 and $100 — never a percentage of the job price, never a commission. (One percentage is allowed: jobs that come through a member's own page cost membership plus 10% of the normal flat fee — 10% of the fee, never of the job price.)

Membership and the founding offer

  • Membership is shown as a choice, never a requirement. Prices: $19/week, $49/month, $490/year.
  • Nothing says membership — or anything else — gets a contractor jobs sooner or ranks them higher.
  • Founding offer: a founder who brings in another contractor earns free membership days — 90 days for the first, +30 for each one after — counted when the new contractor is approved.
  • The offer says free days stop adding once the roster reaches 10 contractors — and that days already earned stay earned.
  • Founding codes look like BUZZ-01 (they run BUZZ-01 to BUZZ-20) — one person, one code, and the page says referral credit follows the code.

Trust and safety

  • After a contractor accepts a job, contact goes through a masked relay — no real phone numbers are shown to either side.
  • Nothing claims Busy Bees checks licenses, insurance, or backgrounds. (It doesn't — flag anything that sounds like it does.) On customer pages, the paperwork badge reads "Paperwork shown" — that's the honest label for it.
  • Sponsor cards are clearly labeled "Sponsored." In the test site they show up on the Almanac pages — never in the middle of making a request.
  • The pretend job offers show job details only — no customer names, phone numbers, or street addresses before a job is accepted and paid for.
  • The "Don't see your trade?" pages say plainly that applying only joins a waitlist — no new trade is live, no jobs are promised, and Busy Bees decides whether a trade ever opens.

Ease of use

  • Every screen has a way Home and a way Back.
  • One question per screen, and only a few choices at a time — nothing feels crowded.
  • Buttons say what they actually do, in plain words.
  • The site never left you stuck with no obvious next step.

How to send your notes back

For each thing you flag, jot down four things:

  • 1. Which part of the site — Customers or For contractors
  • 2. What screen you were on
  • 3. What you tapped or typed
  • 4. What you expected vs. what actually happened

A screenshot helps a lot if you can grab one. Send your notes to Busy Bees, however you normally get in touch — a list in a text is perfect.

For writing them up, use the notes page on the review page — it's a readable sheet you can fill in right on your phone as you go. (A plain-text version, notes.txt, is there too if you'd rather.) Either way, put your name and your review code at the top.

Thank you. This board only works if a regular person can use it without thinking hard. If it worked for you, that matters. If it didn't, that matters more — please say so.
