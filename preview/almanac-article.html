The Cape Cod Yard Almanac · Fall

When to do your fall cleanup

Too early wastes the work. Too late buries it under wet leaves and early snow. Here's the timing that works on Cape Cod.

Wait for most of the leaves — not all of them

The sweet spot is after roughly three-quarters of the leaves are down but before they mat into a wet layer. On the Cape that's usually late October into November, later near the water where oaks hold on. A matted layer left all winter smothers grass and invites mold — that's the thing you're preventing.

What a good fall cleanup covers

  • Leaves off the lawn, beds, and driveway — piled at the curb if you're having them hauled.
  • Perennials cut back (leave some standing for the birds if you like — it's your call, not a rule).
  • Gutters cleared before the freeze, so ice doesn't back up under the shingles.
  • Last mow a touch shorter than summer height.

The curb trick

If raking is the part you don't mind, pile the leaves at the curb and let someone else do the hauling. It's an easy split of the job: you rake, they load and go. On Busy Bees it's called Curbside leaf pickup, under Lawn Care.
