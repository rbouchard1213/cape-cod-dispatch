Busy Bees is one board where local customers ask for yard, home, and hauling help — and the requests come to contractors like you, by text.

The deal, in plain words

  • You only pay when you accept a job — one flat fee, shown in the offer before you decide.
  • No subscription to join. No commission. You keep 100% of the job price — you quote the customer yourself.
  • For example: curbside leaf pickup runs $10 to $60 by pile size, and a standard weekly mowing arrangement is $20.
  • Declining is always free, and passing on a job never counts against you.
  • The fee you see is the fee you pay — it never changes after you accept. Fees run $5 to $100, set by the size of the job.
  • Repeat work: for weekly or seasonal jobs, the flat fee is paid once, when you accept the arrangement — never per visit. One arrangement = one customer, one address, one kind of work. A new season, or a different kind of work, is a new offer with a new fee.
  • If the job dies through no fault of yours, the fee comes back as credit. What qualifies: a bad number, a fake or duplicate lead, a customer who answers nobody within 4 days of you accepting (after you've texted them at least twice through the board), a cancel within 24 hours of you accepting, or a no-show at the door.
  • How credit works: text Busy Bees within 7 days of the problem to claim it or to report a no-show — for a customer who answers nobody, the 7 days run from day 4. The credit equals what you actually paid, after any discount, and sits on your account — it comes off your next charge automatically, whichever comes first, whether that's a lead or your membership bill. Credit never expires and is never paid out as cash. Credits are not discounts — they only replace a fee you already paid. The same rule covers own-page and standing-accept jobs — for an own-page job, the 10% for that job is simply removed from your next bill. If a one-time discount (welcome pick or referral) was on a lead that dies, the discount comes back whole too, good for 30 days from the day the credit lands — a dead lead never uses up part of a multi-lead discount.
  • What doesn't count: a size that's off — you quote the real job on site (two trees that turn out to be ten is a bigger quote, not a credit).
  • Getting the customer, quoting, and losing the job — that's the introduction doing its work.
  • A repeat plan cancelled more than 24 hours after you accepted — before or after the first visit. A job that's a different kind of work than the request said does count as a bad lead.
  • Customers never pay anything. The fee is yours, only on jobs you accept.

How it works

  1. Get the text. A matched job arrives by text: the job, the town, the size, how often it repeats, and the flat fee — all in plain words.
  2. Confirm and pay. Say yes, pay the flat fee through a secure checkout, and only then are the customer's details released to you. Declining is always free.
  3. Do the job. You talk to the customer through the board's private relay — neither phone number is ever shown. You quote the job price yourself, on site.

Good to know

  • Verified discount: show insurance = 5% off the fee · insurance + a second paper (license or business certificate) = 10% off · a license or certificate alone earns the status but 0% off until insurance is shown. Any business paper counts toward verified — we keep a copy on file; we don't check it with the issuer. No paperwork is needed to join the board or get jobs. When a paper expires, the discount ends and verified pauses until you show a current copy.
  • One discount per lead — discounts never combine.
  • Welcome pick (choose one when you join): 50% off your first lead, or 25% off your first 3 leads. Lead fees are never free — discounts are percentages only.
  • Referrals: bring a new contractor and you both get 25% off a lead once they're approved. Bring a new customer and you get 20% off your next lead when their first request is accepted.

Optional extra — your own booking page

Membership is never required for board jobs, and the deal above never changes. Members also get their own Busy Bees page to hand to customers they already have; bookings that come through that page cost membership plus 10% of the normal fee instead of the full fee. The 10% is figured on the normal fee, before any discounts, and figured to the cent — 10% of $25 is $2.50. Fees and discounts are always figured to the cent.

Membership: $19/week · $49/month · $490/year. Membership never buys ranking, ratings, level, or verified status — ever.
