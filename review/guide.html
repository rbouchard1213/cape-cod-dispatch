Busy Bees — Test Site Review Guide

A walkthrough + checklist for Bobby's trusted reviewers

What you're looking at

Busy Bees is a local service dispatch board Bobby is building for Cape Cod. Homeowners request yard and home services for free. Contractors get a text about the job and only pay a small flat fee if they say yes to it.

The test site — button 2 on the review page — is the test version of the site. Everything in it is pretend — fake customers, fake contractors, pretend texts, pretend payments. Nothing you tap is real, and you can't break anything. Poke everything.

What Bobby needs from you: fresh eyes. You're not being tested — the site is. If something confuses you, makes you stop and think, or looks wrong, that's exactly what he needs to hear. "I got lost here" is one of the most useful things you can say.
The deal: Bobby pays $30 when you've gone through the whole guide and checklist and sent your notes back — your choice: $30 cash, or a $35 gift card. Contractors: your review pay is one free month of Busy Bees membership (a $49 value) instead of the cash or gift card. Pay comes in full, all at once when your finished notes are checked in; write your review code at the top of your notes. — just finish within two weeks, by Friday, November 13. Finish within the first week, by Friday, November 6, and that's an extra $10. Find something real that is not on the checklist: $5 for each one (extras top out at $25 per person). One truly exceptional catch earns a $20 bonus on top. (Bobby decides what counts as a real find — the same thing found twice counts once.)

How to start

  • Go back to the review page and tap 2 — Open the test site. It opens right here in your browser, on a phone or a computer — Chrome works best (Edge, Firefox, and Safari are fine too).
  • If you can, do it on your phone — that's how most people will use the real site.
  • There's a row of view buttons that let you jump between the Customer view, the Contractor view, and the Dispatcher view (that's Bobby's seat). You'll use all three.
  • Jot down the time you start. The notes sheet asks for your start and finish times when you're done — any long breaks, just note them; they don't count against you.

The walkthrough — three passes

Pass 1 — Pretend you're a homeowner

  • Start a service request. Pick a town, pick a service, answer the questions one screen at a time.
  • When it asks about size, try the map/box tool and the square-foot entry.
  • Go all the way to the final confirmation screen. Read it like a stranger would.
  • Then look at the Yard Almanac section (yard tips and seasonal guides). It should open right up — no sign-in.

Pass 2 — Pretend you're a contractor

  • Switch to the Contractor view and open the profile. Find the part that says "Your founding code."
  • Try the referral entry: first enter a different contractor's code (like BUZZ-02), then try entering your own code. Your own should be refused.
  • Accept one test job. Decline another one. Both should be easy, and declining should cost nothing.
  • Read the membership section and the founding offer carefully — the checklist below asks about them.

Pass 3 — Sit in Bobby's seat

  • Switch to the Dispatcher view — this is what Bobby sees.
  • Look at how requests come in, how a contractor gets approved, and where the referral codes and free days show up.
  • Open the Test Runs page — it shows the automatic checks the site runs on itself.

The checklist — things that must be true

Tick each one off as you confirm it. If anything below is not true anywhere you find it, that's a flag — note it and tell Bobby.

Words and numbers

  • The name is always Busy Bees — both B's capital, no apostrophe. Flag "Busy Bee's" or lowercase versions.
  • The only phone number anywhere is (508) 258-9899. Flag any other number, anywhere.
  • There is no "emergency" service and no emergency wording anywhere.
  • No home address or street address for Bobby or the business appears anywhere.

Money rules

  • Homeowners are never shown a fee and never asked to pay anything.
  • No screen asks a homeowner for a card or payment details.
  • Contractors pay only when they accept a job. Declining or ignoring a job is always free.
  • Fees are flat amounts between $5 and $100 — never a percentage of the job price, never a commission.

Membership and the founding offer

  • Membership is shown as a choice, never a requirement. Prices: $19/week, $49/month, $490/year.
  • Nothing says membership — or anything else — gets a contractor jobs sooner or ranks them higher.
  • Founding offer: a founder who brings in another contractor earns free membership days — 90 days for the first, +30 for each one after — counted when the new contractor is approved.
  • The offer says free days stop adding once the roster reaches 10 contractors — and that days already earned stay earned.
  • Referral codes look like BUZZ-01 (they run BUZZ-01 to BUZZ-20). A contractor cannot use their own code.

Trust and safety

  • After a contractor accepts a job, contact goes through a masked relay — no real phone numbers are shown to either side.
  • Nothing claims Busy Bees checks licenses, insurance, or backgrounds. (It doesn't — flag anything that sounds like it does.)
  • Sponsor cards are clearly labeled "Sponsored." They show up on the Almanac and area pages only — never in the middle of making a request.
  • The contractor job board shows job details only — no customer names, phone numbers, or street addresses before a job is accepted and paid for.

Ease of use

  • Every screen has a way Home and a way Back.
  • One question per screen, and only a few choices at a time — nothing feels crowded.
  • Buttons say what they actually do, in plain words.
  • The site never left you stuck with no obvious next step.

How to send your notes back

For each thing you flag, jot down four things:

  • 1. Which view — Customer, Contractor, or Dispatcher
  • 2. What screen you were on
  • 3. What you tapped or typed
  • 4. What you expected vs. what actually happened

A screenshot helps a lot if you can grab one. Send your notes to Bobby however you normally reach him — a list in a text is perfect.

Thank you. This board only works if a regular person can use it without thinking hard. If it worked for you, that matters. If it didn't, that matters more — please say so.
