Words and numbers

1Everywhere I looked, the name was "Busy Bees" — never "Busy Bee's," never lowercase. /
2The only phone number I saw anywhere was (508) 258-9899. /
3I never saw "emergency" offered as a service or a promise. /
4I never saw a home or street address for the owner or the business. /

Money rules

5As a homeowner, I was never shown a fee. /
6As a homeowner, I was never asked for a card or any payment. /
7As a contractor, declining or ignoring a job cost nothing. /
8Every fee I saw was a flat amount between $5 and $100 — never a percentage of the job price, never a commission. (The one percentage anywhere: a member's own-page bookings cost 10% of the flat fee — of the fee, not of the job price.) /

Membership and the founding offer

9Membership was always shown as a choice, never a requirement. /
10The membership prices were $19/week, $49/month, $490/year. /
11Nothing said paying members get jobs sooner or rank higher. /
12The founding offer said 90 free days for a first referral, +30 for each one after, counted when the new contractor is approved. /
13The founding offer said free days stop adding at 10 contractors — and days already earned stay earned. /
14The founding page showed an example founding code (BUZZ-01), said one person gets one code, and said referral credit follows the code. There was nowhere in the test site to type a code in. /

Trust and safety

15After accepting a job, contact stayed masked — I never saw a real phone number. /
16Nothing claimed Busy Bees checks licenses, insurance, or backgrounds. /
17Sponsor cards were labeled "Sponsored" and never showed up in the middle of making a request. /
18The pretend job offers showed no customer names, phone numbers, or street addresses before a job is accepted and paid for. /

Ease of use

19Every screen had a way Home and a way Back. /
20Each screen asked one thing at a time, with only a few choices. /
21I never got stuck with no obvious next step. /

Overall

22I could use this site on my own, without help. /
23I would trust this site to book a real job. /
24The "Don't see your trade?" pages made it clear that applying only joins a waitlist — no new trade is live, no jobs are promised, and Busy Bees decides whether a trade ever opens. /
