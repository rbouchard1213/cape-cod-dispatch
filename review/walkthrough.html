Busy Bees Test Site Walkthrough — Step-by-Step Guide

What this is: a checklist for trying out the new Busy Bees board before it goes live. Go in order, top to bottom. Tick off each part as you finish it. When every part is ticked, this walkthrough is done — Busy Bees takes your notes from there and decides when the new board goes live.

Before you start — read this first

  • The test site is a practice copy, not the real site. Nothing in it is real: no real customers, no real contractors, no real texts, no real payments. The names, jobs, and numbers in it are made-up examples.
  • Open the test site from the review page — button 2, "Open the test site." It opens in the phone layout, which is how most people will see it.
  • You can't change the test site yourself — and you don't need to. When you spot something that should change, write it in your notes in plain words ("on the cart screen, the button is hard to tap") and send the notes to Busy Bees.
  • Jot problems down as you find them, or save up a list. Either way works. If something looks broken, note what you tapped right before it broke.
  • Don't worry about these — they don't go live: the made-up example data, and the map box picture (see Part 3). Judge the flow and the wording, not those.
  • Some real-build pieces are not in the test site at all — a working contractor-choice screen, contractor profiles, a lead inbox, a job board, and a breakeven calculator. Where a part below describes one of these, it says so plainly. Don't flag them as broken or missing — they were never put in the test site. Do flag it if the test site's own words about them are confusing.

Plain words used in this guide:

  • Cart = the list of jobs you're asking about, before you send anything.
  • Lead fee = what a contractor pays Busy Bees when they accept a job. Customers never see fees and never pay them.
  • Roster = the approved list of contractors on the board.

Part 1 — Customer flow, as a guest (no sign-in)

This is the most important part. A guest is someone who just opens the site and asks for work, without an account.

  1. Open the test site. You land on the preview home page: a "Give us a buzz" welcome, two big buttons (Request a local pro, and I'm a contractor), a short "How it works" card, and below that a menu in two groups — Customers and For contractors. The areas live under "Areas we serve" in the Customers group.
  2. Tap Request a local pro.
  3. Pick a town from the town grid.
  4. Pick a category (there are 7: Tree Work, Lawn Care, Landscaping, Storm Cleanup, Snow Removal, Heavy Hauling, Pressure Washing).
  5. Pick a job inside that category.
  6. If it asks for a size, pick one.
  7. Fill in the details screen (name, phone, notes).
  8. Look at the confirmation screen. It should show a big text button and make it obvious this is a request, not an instant booking — the pro confirms first. (In the test site the text is only pretend. Nothing is actually sent.)

Check as you go:

  • Every screen has a Back button and a Home link in the header.
  • You can tell where you are on every screen (the path cue).
  • No screen makes you scroll forever to find the button.
  • No screen has more than 8 choices on it.
  • The words are plain and the buttons are big enough to tap easily. Think: could an older person do this without help?
  • The confirmation screen has a Back to home link.

Do this whole guest flow at least once. Then repeat steps 2–8 for one job from each of the other 6 categories — you don't need every single service, just one per category, so all 7 get covered.

Part 2 — Customer flow, signed in (the account pages)

The test site's signed-in side is a pair of pretend account pages — there is no real sign-in and no working contractor-choice screen in the test site. Here is what to try:

  1. From the home page's Customers group, open Your account — free & premium. Read what a free account holds: saved details, request history, one-tap re-requests, tracking, and "pick your contractor."
  2. Tap the pretend sign-in to reach the account home (a made-up customer, Jane). Check her saved details, her past requests with "Request again" links, and her main-contractor card (Example Tree Co, for tree work).
  3. Read the Premium panel: $20/month after a 30-day free trial, for a main contractor per kind of work plus preference extras. Premium is not for sale yet — judge the wording only.

Know before you judge: in the real build, a signed-in customer also gets a contractor-choice question during a request — a previous contractor, a site-recommended one, or discovering one from a matching list. That working choice screen is not in the test site; the account pages only describe it. Don't flag the missing screen — flag it only if the account pages explain it badly.

Check as you go:

  • The free account and Premium are easy to tell apart, and what Premium adds is plain.
  • Nothing says an account is needed to send a request — requesting is always free, with or without one.
  • Nothing here promises a contractor is available or how fast they'll come.

Part 3 — Size screens: calculator, square feet, and the map box

Size is where jobs get their price tier, so try all three ways of giving a size.

  1. Pick a size from the list. Try a job with size bands (for example mowing or mulch) and check the band words make sense.
  2. Type a square footage. Look for "Know your square footage? Enter it" on a size screen, type a number, and check the board puts it in the right tier. Boundary rule to know: an exact boundary number rounds DOWN to the lower tier.
  3. Open the map box. On a size screen where it appears, tap to open it, trace an area, and check the square-foot number fills into the field. Important: the map picture in the test site is a schematic lot grid, not real satellite photos, because the test site has no live map connection. The real site gets real satellite imagery to trace. Judge whether the box opens, traces, fills the number, and closes without breaking the flow — not the picture itself.
  4. Check the tips and reference sizes on the size screen (for example, a parking space is about 160 sq ft, and 1 yard of mulch covers about 100 sq ft).

Check as you go:

  • Typing a number always works, even if the map box fails (manual fallback).
  • The size words say "area to work, not lot size" where it matters.
  • The map box never slows down or blocks the main flow.

Part 4 — Cart (more than one job at once)

  1. Add a job to the cart, then go back and add a second, different job.
  2. Open the cart (top-right corner of the screen).
  3. Check every item shows which service, which variant, and which size — not just the group name. For example, if you picked a grouped button like "Driveways, patios & decks," the cart must say which one you actually picked.
  4. Check the cart total adds the jobs up.
  5. After sending, check the confirmation screen says, in plain words: Busy Bees matches the request to a local pro who does this work in your town, the pro confirms before anything is final, you talk through the private relay (your real number stays hidden), and if every matched contractor passes, Busy Bees keeps working on it and lets you know — you are never just left hanging.

Check as you go:

  • Cart button is in the top-right corner, on phone and on every screen.
  • Each cart item shows its exact variant and size.
  • The cart never shows a fee to the customer.

Part 5 — "Something else? Tell us"

  1. Find the Something else? Tell us link under the category grid.
  2. Describe a job in your own words, add name and phone, and send it.
  3. Check that no price tier is shown for it — Busy Bees assigns the tier later, and the contractor confirms it before anything is paid.

Check as you go:

  • The link is easy to find, both under the categories and at the bottom of service screens.
  • Nothing here invents a service or a price for a job you described.

Part 6 — Wording pass (whole site)

Go back over the screens you've already tried, this time reading only the words.

Check as you go:

  • The main buttons say Request a local pro, not vague words like "Get service" or "Book a service."
  • A contractor's own page says Request this contractor plus the service.
  • Under the main button, a helper line says it's free to send, the pro confirms first, and it's a request, not an instant booking.
  • Nothing says "emergency," nothing promises a fast response, and nothing claims anyone is insured.
  • No customer screen shows a fee, a dollar amount, or the word "subscription."
  • The owner's separate contracting business name (Bob of All Trades) and the owner's personal name appear nowhere customer-facing.

Part 7 — Contractor side

The contractor side of the test site is a set of explainer pages plus two sample job offers. There are no contractor profiles, no lead inbox, and no job board in the test site — those are real-build pieces. Work through the pages that are there:

  1. Open Join & how job offers work and read it end to end: the deal (pay only on accept, flat fee, no commission, decline always free), how an offer arrives by text, what happens if a job dies through no fault of the contractor (the fee comes back as account credit), and the optional own-page extra.
  2. Open See a pretend job offer. There are two samples: a one-time tree removal (flat fee $30) and a weekly mowing arrangement (fee paid once per arrangement, never per visit). Tap Accept on one and read the accepted screen; go back and tap Decline on the other and read the declined screen.
  3. Open Levels & verified. Check it explains, in plain words: levels grow with jobs completed; verified contractors get the first look; standing-accept fires at the end of the first look; the trust score stays private; and the paperwork rules — customers see "Paperwork shown," insurance alone earns 5% off, insurance plus a second paper of a different kind earns 10%, a license or certificate alone earns the status but no discount, and Busy Bees keeps a copy on file without checking it with the issuer.
  4. Open Founding codes and check the founding offer wording (see the checklist lines about it).
  5. Open Don't see your trade? — the future-trades application (three short screens). It is a process, not a live service: a contractor in a trade Busy Bees doesn't carry yet (the examples are auto repair and HVAC) can apply to a waitlist. Applying is free, promises no jobs, and opens nothing automatically — Busy Bees reviews every application personally, the back-office AI only sorts and counts, and a trade is normally only designed once three approved contractors offer it in the same area.

Check as you go:

  • Both sample offers show the job, town, size, how often it repeats (when it repeats), and the flat fee — before any accept.
  • Neither sample offer shows a customer name, street address, or phone number.
  • Declining is stated as free everywhere it comes up, and never counts against the contractor.
  • Nothing promises how fast jobs or customers will come.
  • The future-trades screens never make it sound like a new trade is open or that applying guarantees anything.

Part 8 — Membership and a member's own page

Members get their own Busy Bees page, where their own customers request them directly. In the test site this is two pages:

  1. Open Membership — your own page. Check the price, stated plainly: $19/week · $49/month · $490/year, renewing automatically until cancel, cancel anytime in two taps, page stays up to the end of the paid period, no refunds for unused time.
  2. Check the own-page maths on the same page: own-page bookings cost membership plus 10% of the normal fee — the page's own examples are a $30 fee becoming $3, and 10% of $25 being $2.50, figured to the cent. The 10% amounts are tallied and billed with the membership, not charged job by job. If the member declines an own-page request, it closes, the customer is asked before it can go to the board, and the member owes no 10% for it.
  3. Check board-matched jobs still cost the full fee for everyone, member or not, and that membership never buys ranking, ratings, level, or verified status.
  4. Tap the pretend sign-up and read the member welcome screen (the page switched on).
  5. Open A member's own page (Example Tree Co) — from the home page's Customers group or the link on the membership page. Check the banner: you're requesting Example Tree Co directly, they're already chosen, and it's still a request — they confirm first, contact stays masked, and if they decline, the request closes there and Busy Bees asks you before sending it to the board. Pick a kind of work and note the flow continues one question at a time, like the normal request flow.

Not in the test site: a breakeven calculator and plan-switching controls are design pieces for the real build — they are not in the test site, so don't flag them as missing or broken.

Check as you go:

  • The member page flow reads as easily as the normal customer flow.
  • The prices on the page ($19 / $49 / $490) are the real, decided prices — Busy Bees set them on October 27. Membership itself is not for sale yet (a legal review comes first), so your job is just to check the maths and the wording.

Part 9 — Areas and pre-launch areas

  1. Open Areas we serve from the home page's Customers group. The whole thing is one page with four area cards.
  2. On the Cape Cod — LIVE card, check the 8 towns are right: Yarmouth, Hyannis/Barnstable, Dennis, Sandwich, Chatham, Harwich, Mashpee, Brewster.
  3. Check the three pre-launch cards on the same page:
    • South Shore: "opening soon," 2 of 3 contractors on the roster (a pretend count), and a note that contractors there can already join the list.
    • Western Mass: "opening soon," 1 of 3 (a pretend count), and no going-live date — never a made-up date.
    • Rhode Island: "opening soon," 2 of 3 (a pretend count).
  4. Check every pre-launch card says the same rule in plain words: the area opens when 3 local contractors are on the roster.

Check as you go:

  • Every counter is labelled a pretend count in this test version — on the real site, counters only ever count contractors on the roster, never padded numbers.
  • No pre-launch card promises a date.
  • The Home link works from the areas page.

Part 10 — Home link sweep (and where suggestions live)

  1. Know this first: the test site has no separate Suggestions page. Suggestions go in your notes — Busy Bees reads every one and decides. If you went looking for a suggestions page of your own, note that in your notes; it helps Busy Bees.
  2. Go through every screen you can find and check the Home link is in the header on every single one, and the confirmation screen has Back to home.

Part 11 — Desktop pass (the owner's laptop check — you can skip this part)

This part is the owner's own check, done later on the owner's laptop after the phone pass — you can skip it. For the record: on a wide screen, only the arrangement gets checked (columns, spacing, layout). Desktop-only arrangement tweaks must NOT change the phone view. The words, order, and services are shared across both, so a wording change on one would change the other too.

Part 12 — Decisions at the end

These are open items for the owner to decide — not for you. They're listed so you know what's still unsettled. You don't need to pick anything. If you have an opinion on any of them, put it in your notes.

  1. Membership prices — decided. Busy Bees set them on October 27: $19/week, $49/month, $490/year. Membership is not for sale until the legal review clears. (Also settled: the 10% applies to own-page bookings only; board-matched jobs cost the full flat fee.)
  2. Range fees. Some fees are ranges (for example tree trimming $15–$30). Every range endpoint needs a value from the 13 Stripe amounts ($5–$100), unless the owner approves the rule that the dispatcher may use any of the 13 amounts.
  3. Snow flow + plow split. The reordered snow flow and the split plow options (one-time vs. seasonal) are built into the test site for you to try — the owner still gives the final yes before the real build copies them.
  4. Bush routing + roof-wash fee. Bush jobs routing through the "how often" screen is built into the test site to try; the roof-wash fee is still the owner's to set.
  5. Spec values (proposed, not final). Level bands (New 0–4 / Growing 5–19 / Established 20–49 / Trusted 50+), approve-window minutes (15 / 30 / 45 / 60, 1 hour max), cart level discount (Trusted 10% / Established 5% / else 0%), and peak promo windows. Busy Bees decides which to keep and which to change.
  6. Whole-property vs cart-sum rule. A whole-property cleanout is one fee, but adding house + driveway + deck separately in the cart sums higher. the owner rules which one wins when they overlap.
  7. Attorney review. the owner approves the Terms Rev 3 direction, and a Massachusetts attorney reviews them before anything launches.

When you're done

You are done when:

  • Parts 1–10 are all ticked.
  • Every problem or idea you found is written in your notes, and the notes are sent to Busy Bees.
  • Any opinions you have — including on the open items in Part 12 — are in your notes.
  • Part 11 is skipped — that's the owner's own laptop check.

Then tell Busy Bees you're done and send your notes. What happens next is the Busy Bees side of it: once everything checks out, the new board goes live behind a maintenance page — maintenance page up, new board swapped in and checked, maintenance page down, old site ready to put back if anything looks wrong.

Part 13 — Almanac + sponsor ads (added Oct 30, 2026)

The free gardening section and the sponsor cards went into the test site on Oct 30. Check them like this:

  1. Open The Cape Cod Yard Almanac from the home page's Customers group. It must open with NO sign-in wall — anybody can read it.
  2. On the Almanac page, count the sponsor cards: 2. Cards must say "Sponsored — Local Partner" and show garden-type stores only (nurseries, garden centers, hardware) — never a contractor.
  3. Open the one finished article, "When to do your fall cleanup." It has 1 sponsor card, and it ends with ONE calm button pointing to "Request a local pro" — nothing pushy, no pop-ups, nothing moving. The other topics on the Almanac page are marked "Coming soon" — they should NOT open the fall-cleanup article.
  4. Run one full request (guest) start to finish and confirm: zero ads anywhere in the request flow.
  5. Anything off — wrong count, a card in the wrong place, wording that feels pushy — note the screen name and put it in your notes for Busy Bees, like the other parts.

Done when: counts check out on the Almanac page and the one article, and the request flow is clean.
