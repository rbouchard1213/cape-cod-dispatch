What's New

Every change to Busy Bees, posted here in plain words — what changed, and what it means for you. Newest first.

This is the practice copy of the page. On the real site this page is public, and every change lands here as it ships — nothing quiet, nothing buried.

October 31, 2026

The Hive — a back office for members

Contractors now have their own back office, called The Hive: their customers, estimates, jobs, and invoices in one place — fed by the jobs they win on the board, so a job they accept shows up in their own records on its own. It even keeps track of what each job made them.

Invoices run through the contractor's own payment account: money goes straight from the customer to the contractor, and Busy Bees never holds job money and never takes a cut of it. Coming for members after that: Lead Hunter, an optional extra that finds local work looking for a pro. It's not on sale yet.

October 31, 2026

Your work saves as you go

Forms and notes on this site now save on your own device while you type. Close a page halfway through a request and you can pick up right where you left off — nothing gets lost because a page closed.

October 31, 2026

A privacy page in plain words

Busy Bees added a privacy page that says exactly what this site counts and why — visits are counted in Busy Bees' own system, with no outside trackers and no ad cookies — and it carries a switch to turn the counting off in your browser.

October 30, 2026

Plainer words, on purpose

We cleaned up the board's wording: out went words that promised more than we do — no "vetted" claims, no fast-response promises. Here, "verified" means one thing only: a contractor's business paperwork is on file. Nothing more is implied, ever.

October 30, 2026

Where this starts — how Busy Bees works

Busy Bees is a local dispatch board for Cape Cod. A customer picks a town, answers a few plain questions, and sends one simple request — free, always. Local contractors get matched job offers by text and pay one flat fee only when they accept a job; saying no is always free, and no contractor ever pays a percentage of the job.

This page starts here. Every change from now on gets posted above, in the open.
